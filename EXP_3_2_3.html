Chapter 2-3

Superposition in dispersive media

When ω and k are no longer linearly related, different frequencies travel at different speeds — the envelope and carrier decouple, and pulses spread.

Key misconception to address first: when a wave crosses a boundary into a new medium, only its wavelength changes — not its frequency. Think of a hammer hitting a wall: the rhythm of blows (frequency) is set by the source and never changes. The wall only controls how it transmits each blow (wavelength/phase speed).

Chapter knowledge check multiple choice

A dispersive medium has ω = 3k + 0.06k³. Two waves have k₁ = 2 rad/m and k₂ = 2.5 rad/m. Which statement correctly compares their phase speeds and the group speed of the superposition?

Computing: ω₁ = 3(2)+0.06(8) = 6.48, so vₚ₁ = 6.48/2 = 3.24 m/s. ω₂ = 3(2.5)+0.06(15.625) = 8.4375, so vₚ₂ = 8.4375/2.5 = 3.375 m/s. Group speed: uₘ = Δω/Δk = (8.4375−6.48)/(2.5−2) = 1.9575/0.5 = 3.915 m/s. All three speeds are different — the hallmark of a dispersive medium.
